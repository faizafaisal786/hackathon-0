A Timeless Warning from Bertrand Russell and Albert Einstein In July 1955, at the dawn of the nuclear age, two of the twentieth century’s most influential thinkers—Bertrand Russell and Albert Einstein—issued a profound appeal to humanity. Known as the Russell–Einstein Manifesto, their statement confronted a stark reality: technological progress had given humanity the power not only to transform the world but also to destroy it. The world they addressed was emerging from the devastation of World War II and entering the tense rivalries of the Cold War. Nuclear weapons had introduced a new and terrifying dimension to human conflict. For the first time in history, the survival of civilization itself could be threatened by decisions made by nations locked in ideological competition. Russell and Einstein did not speak as politicians or diplomats. They spoke as human beings addressing other human beings. Their message was both simple and profound: “There lies before us, if we choose, continual progress in happiness, knowledge, and wisdom.
