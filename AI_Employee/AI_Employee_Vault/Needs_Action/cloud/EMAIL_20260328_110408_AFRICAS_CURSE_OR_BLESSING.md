Africa is once again at the center of the world’s attention—not because of its ideas, its people, or its institutions, but because of what lies beneath its soil. From lithium and cobalt to copper, manganese, and rare earths, the continent holds a decisive share of the minerals powering the global energy transition, digital revolution, and modern defense systems. The global scramble for critical minerals has arrived in Africa with urgency—and with danger. History should make us cautious. Africa knows this story well. In the era of gold, ivory, rubber, and oil, global demand translated not into shared prosperity but into extraction without transformation, wealth without development, and growth without dignity. Today’s minerals are branded “green” and “future-facing,” but the political economy surrounding them feels uncomfortably familiar. The world presents the scramble as inevitable. Electric vehicles, renewable energy, artificial intelligence, and advanced weapons systems all depend on mineral-intensive supply chains.
