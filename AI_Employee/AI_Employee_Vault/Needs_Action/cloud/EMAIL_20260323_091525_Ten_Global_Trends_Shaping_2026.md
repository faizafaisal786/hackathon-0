Navigating a World at the Edge The approach to 2026 finds the world in a condition best described not as transition, but as tension. The international system is under strain from multiple directions at once—geopolitical rivalry, technological acceleration, social fracture, ecological stress, and institutional fatigue. Unlike previous eras, these forces are not unfolding sequentially. They are converging, interacting, and amplifying one another. What follows is not a prediction of inevitabilities, but a map of pressures. Together, these ten global trends define the operating environment of 2026—and the narrowing margin for error within it. The first trend is the fragmentation of the world order. The era of confident globalization has given way to de-globalization, bloc formation, and transactional geopolitics. Rules once taken for granted—free trade, multilateral cooperation, diplomatic restraint—are increasingly subordinated to power, security, and national interest.
