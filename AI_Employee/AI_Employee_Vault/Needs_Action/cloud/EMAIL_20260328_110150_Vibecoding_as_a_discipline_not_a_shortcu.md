by Thomas Thorsell-Arntsen Imagine this for a moment. You’re working on a new digital product. The experience feels right early. Everyone agrees on the flow. Decisions stick. There’s less friction between design and development. Fewer misunderstandings. Fewer rewrites. Progress feels calm. You’re still moving fast, but not recklessly. And most importantly, what you build actually resembles what you imagined. This is what vibecoding looks like when it’s done properly. And yes, it’s something you can learn. Feel the vibe (for real this time) Vibe-coding has moved quickly from curiosity to capability. Instead of starting with technical constraints, teams begin with experience. What the user sees. How they move through a product. What decisions they make along the way. Platforms like Lovable make this tangible by enabling teams to design, test, and iterate on real user experiences quickly. Not wireframes. Not isolated user flows. Actual, usable products. The result is faster learning, better alignment, and earlier validation, especially when designers, product managers, and developers work together instead of in sequence.
