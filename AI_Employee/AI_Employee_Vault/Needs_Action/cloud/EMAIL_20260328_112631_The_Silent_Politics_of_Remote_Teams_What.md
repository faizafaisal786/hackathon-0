Remote work promises flexibility, freedom, and fairness. No cubicles. No office gossip. No visible hierarchy. But the truth is uncomfortable: Office politics didn’t disappear. They just became quieter. In remote teams, power doesn’t shout. It whispers—through emails, meeting invites, Slack channels, and “who gets looped in.” And many professionals feel it, even if they can’t explain it. 1. Visibility Matters More Than Merit In remote teams, being seen often matters more than being useful. Employees who: Speak frequently in meetings Respond instantly on chat Stay “online” longer …are often perceived as more committed. Meanwhile, someone who: Delivers quality work Avoids unnecessary meetings Focuses deeply instead of talking loudly …may quietly get ignored. Corporate Example Two employees deliver the same results. One talks more in meetings. The other works silently. Guess who gets noticed first? This creates frustration and silent resentment.
