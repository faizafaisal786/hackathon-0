Christ Is the Rock on Which We Stand Beloved in Christ, We find ourselves living in an hour when many experience, whether consciously or quietly, that something foundational is trembling. In our own nation, the United States of America, public trust has eroded, political discourse has hardened into suspicion, and neighbors increasingly view one another through lenses of ideology rather than shared humanity. Around the world, war, displacement, unfinished histories of injustice, fragile states, and widening inequality deepen instability. Technology accelerates at breathtaking speed while moral formation struggles to keep pace. Beneath these visible tremors lies something more profound: a disturbance in our sense of ultimate ground. The theologian Paul Tillich described such moments as “the shaking of the foundations. He did not refer merely to political instability or cultural change. He meant that the structures to which human beings attach ultimate trust—nation, progress, markets, ideology, even religious forms—are revealed to be less ultimate than we supposed.
